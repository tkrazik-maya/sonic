Reading List: Sonic — Deities, Sound, and the Kirtan Fusion Practice

Curated for the specific thing you're building: the Sonic/kirtan-push instrument, the energy-registry system (Saraswati, Shiva, Ganesha, Krishna/Hanuman, Durga), and the Krishna Das-style song-pairing practice. Not a general survey of Hindu music or philosophy — each entry ties directly to a decision the project depends on.

Organized by the vein it opens up. Section 1 is the practice itself — read this first, since it's the actual method you're extending. Sections 2–3 are the technical substrate behind the energy matrix (docs/sonic-energy-matrix.xlsx in the sonic repo). Section 4 is primary scripture, useful if you want to go straight to the source of a specific stotra.

One filter applied throughout: primary sources and practitioner accounts over secondhand summary, wherever a good one exists.

1. The lineage and the fusion practice

This is the actual method behind the "pair a famous song with a complementary kirtan" concept — not an analogy to it. Krishna Das's own path, and the guru at the center of it, are the reason Krishna and Hanuman merged into one bank in the project's energy registry rather than staying separate.

Chants of a Lifetime: Searching for a Heart of Gold
Krishna Das · Hay House, 2010 · ~200pp, book + CD
His own memoir — the New York Times called him "the chant master of American yoga." Traces the exact arc this project keeps referencing: a rock 'n' roll kid who meets Ram Dass, goes to India, meets Neem Karoli Baba, and comes back years later fusing that devotion with Western musical sensibility. This is the primary source for the whole song-pairing concept, not a secondary account of it. Read this first.
Primary sourceDirect method
Be Here Now
Ram Dass · Lama Foundation, 1971 · ~400pp
The foundational text of the whole lineage — Ram Dass's account of leaving Harvard psychology behind to find Neem Karoli Baba in India. This is the book Krishna Das heard about in 1968 that sent him to India in the first place. Explains why the project's own repo description ("Neem Karoli / Ram Dass lineage") is doing real work, not just flavor text.
FoundationalCountercultural classic
Miracle of Love: Stories about Neem Karoli Baba
Ram Dass (compiler) · Dutton, 1979 · ~500pp
A collected oral history of Neem Karoli Baba from dozens of devotees — the guru both Ram Dass and Krishna Das orbit. Gives direct texture on why devotion in this lineage doesn't separate Krishna, Hanuman, and other deities into competing tracks — it's read as one continuous practice of love. Explains the Krishna/Hanuman merge in the energy registry from the inside.

2. The tantric chakra / bija-mantra / sound system

The primary source behind the matrix's Bija Mantra, Chakra, and Element columns. Most yoga-studio-circuit "chakra charts" you'll find online are diluted several generations from this text — worth going to the root once, even if the language is dense.

The Serpent Power: The Secrets of Tantric and Shaktic Yoga
Arthur Avalon (Sir John Woodroffe) · orig. 1919, Dover ed. ISBN 9780486230580 · ~500pp
Woodroffe's translation and commentary on the Sat-Cakra-Nirupana ("Description of the Six Chakras") and the shorter Paduka-Panchaka — the actual Sanskrit tantric texts that assign a bija mantra, presiding deity, and element to each chakra. This is the primary source the matrix's chakra/bija/element columns trace back to, several removes down. Dense and archaic in places, but it's the real document rather than a modern summary of it. The one to own if you want the matrix's foundation, not just its output.
Primary sourceDense
Kundalini Tantra
Swami Satyananda Saraswati · Bihar School of Yoga, 1984 · ~450pp
A modern, practice-oriented synthesis of the same chakra/bija/deity system, written for a yoga-practitioner audience rather than a Sanskritist one. Easier entry point than Woodroffe if the archaic translation style is a barrier — read this first, then go to The Serpent Power if you want the primary text underneath it.
AccessiblePractice-oriented

3. Raga, rasa, and time-of-day theory

The reference discipline behind the matrix's Raga, Rasa, and Time/Thaat columns — this is where the Bhairav/Shiva, Yaman/Krishna, Hamsadhwani/Ganesha, and Durga raga associations actually come from, with the musicological detail the web-search summaries in the matrix necessarily compressed.

The Raga Guide: A Survey of 74 Hindustani Ragas
Joep Bor, Suvarnalata Rao, Wim van der Meer, Jane Harvey · Nimbus Records, 1999 · book + 4 CDs
The standard modern reference for raga-by-raga detail: time of day, mood/rasa, ascent-descent structure, historical notes — with recordings by Hariprasad Chaurasia (flute), Buddhadev DasGupta (sarod), and others so you can hear each raga alongside reading about it. Directly covers Bhairav (Shiva), Yaman (Krishna), and Durga — the exact ragas in the project's matrix. The single best next step if you want to verify or deepen any raga row in the matrix.
ReferenceIncludes recordings
Raga Mala: The Autobiography of Ravi Shankar
Ravi Shankar, ed. George Harrison, with Oliver Craske · Genesis Publications, 1997 / Welcome Rain, 1999 · ~350pp
A working musician's lived account of raga discipline, guru-shishya training, and what it actually feels like to build a life around this tradition — narrated in the "raga mala" style itself (one recollection sparking the next, always circling back to a theme). Less technical than The Raga Guide, more useful for the felt sense of what you're building toward as a performer.
Practitioner memoirNarrative

4. Primary scripture

If you want to go straight to the source rather than a secondary description — this is where the Argala Stotram itself lives, and where Krishna's own teaching on bhakti (devotion) comes from.

In Praise of the Goddess: The Devimahatmya and Its Meaning
Devadatta Kali (trans.) · Nicolas-Hays, 2003 · ~350pp
A modern translation and commentary on the Devi Mahatmya (also called Durga Saptashati) — the scripture the Argala Stotram is drawn from, the text that started the whole open-registry pivot in this project. The translator, an initiate in the Ramakrishna-Vivekananda lineage, combines Western scholarly apparatus with an insider's devotional read. Read the Argala Stotram itself here, in context, rather than as an isolated YouTube clip.
Primary scriptureSource of Argala Stotram
The Bhagavad Gita
trans. Eknath Easwaran · Nilgiri Press, 2007 (widely available) · ~300pp
Krishna's own teaching on bhakti yoga — the path of devotion — delivered as a dialogue rather than doctrine. Easwaran's translation is the most commonly recommended accessible entry point (clear modern English, solid introduction, no Sanskrit required). Directly relevant to the Krishna half of the merged Krishna/Hanuman bank.
