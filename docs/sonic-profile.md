# Sonic profile — corpus sourcing spec

Source: Thomas's own sonic-identity self-analysis (childhood sound memory → Queen → Deftones/Eno/Jon Hopkins → Krishna Das), used to filter what goes into the vinyl corpus and what the Kirtan drone/tala layers should sound like.

## The profile

Warm, low-frequency, chest-resonant, tactile, immersive. Music as environment, not background — a "grounding sanctuary" the listener steps inside of.

- **Frequency:** low-to-mid emphasis. Deep baritone vocals, harmonium drone, tabla bass. No sharp transients.
- **Vocal:** gravelly, communal, slow-building, close-mic intimacy or wall-of-voices call-and-response. Not solo/operatic, not high-pitched.
- **Rhythm:** tactile, human-hands percussion (tabla, clay drums). Vilambit (slow) to madhya (medium) tempo. No fast/erratic time signatures.
- **Space:** "wrapped" soundstage — the track builds a room you're inside of, not a spotlight aimed at you.

## Deity/acoustic mapping — the key nuance

Traditional Saraswati music (air/water element — bright, fast, sitar/veena-driven, intellectually complex) does **not** match this profile, even though Thomas is drawn to Saraswati as an archetype. What he actually responds to is Saraswati sung through **Shiva / Ganesha / Hanuman** register — earth/fire, drone-anchored, driving tabla. Krishna Das's *Saraswati* works precisely because he delivers an air/water deity through an earth/fire arrangement.

Implication for repertoire: don't source purely by deity name. Source by the instrumentation table below, then apply deity/lyric on top.

## What to look for vs. avoid

| | Warm (source this) | Cool/airy (avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| Instrumentation | Harmonium, tabla, bansuri (low register), cello, tanpura drone | Sitar, veena, high-pitched violin, fast flute, cymbals |
| Vocal style | Deep, gravelly, chanting, communal, slow-building | High-pitched, operatic, fast-paced, sharp, solo-focused |
| Tempo | Vilambit (slow) to madhya (medium) | Drut (fast) or erratic/changing time signatures |

## Reference artists (acoustic fit confirmed against the profile)

- **Krishna Das** — the anchor reference. Deep harmonium drone + driving tabla pulse.
- **Nina Rao** — grounded, earthy vocal tone, heavy electric bass under harmonium.
- **Miten (with Deva Premal)** — close, textured, weathered baritone; slow, unhurried.
- **Bhagavan Das** — raw, unpolished chest resonance, heavy gravelly low end.
- **Shyamdas** — traditional harmonium, predictable call-and-response, zero digital sheen.
- **Jai Uttal** — relentless heartbeat drum pulse, rock-solid harmonium line (his heavier tracks, not the higher-register material).

Secular cross-reference for the same wiring: Brian Eno (static/designed room), Jon Hopkins (tactile organic electronic percussion), Elbow/Guy Garvey (chest-resonant baritone + communal choir swell), Deftones (close-mic intimacy + immersive atmospheric wall of sound).

## Applying this to the build

- **Vinyl corpus sourcing** ([#4](https://github.com/tkrazik-maya/sonic/issues/4), [#6](https://github.com/tkrazik-maya/sonic/issues/6)): prioritize records from the reference artists above, or records matching the instrumentation table, over classical Saraswati/sitar-forward records. A RAVE model trained on bright/fast source material will generate timbre in the wrong direction for this project.
- **Kirtan drone layer** ([#14](https://github.com/tkrazik-maya/sonic/issues/14)): root the MIDI synth drone in low register, sustained, harmonium-like — not a bright pad.
- **Kirtan tala layer** ([#15](https://github.com/tkrazik-maya/sonic/issues/15)): pull tabla/hand-drum stems, vilambit-to-madhya tempo range, avoid fast/erratic cycles.
- **Call-and-response** ([#16](https://github.com/tkrazik-maya/sonic/issues/16)): favor deep, communal-voice AI response timbre over bright/solo.
